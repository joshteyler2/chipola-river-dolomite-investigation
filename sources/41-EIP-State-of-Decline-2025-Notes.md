**Checked:** September 2, 2026

## Official source

- Report title: *State of Decline: Cuts to State Environmental Agencies Compound Damage from the Dismantling of EPA*
- Publisher: Environmental Integrity Project
- Publication date: December 10, 2025
- [Official report page and download](https://environmentalintegrity.org/reports/state-of-decline/)

The report is linked at its official host instead of being mirrored here. These notes identify the exact figures and location used in this repository.

## Figures used

PDF page 19, in the staffing-reduction table and accompanying text, reports:

| Jurisdiction/measure | FY2010 | FY2024 | Change | Percent |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Florida pollution-control/environmental-agency staff | 2,096 | 1,702 | −394 | −19% |
| North Carolina staff decline | — | — | −386 | −32% |

The report identifies Florida as having the largest **absolute number** of eliminated state environmental-agency positions and North Carolina as having the largest **percentage** decline. The period is 2010–2024, not 2010–2025.

## Use and limitations

- The report analyzes full-time-equivalent staffing using state budget and staffing sources described in its methods/appendix.
- It supports a statewide capacity concern, not a finding about how many inspectors or biologists were assigned to Dolomite, Arcosa, or the 2012 study.
- Staffing totals do not, by themselves, prove political motive, inadequate performance in a particular case, or causation of the reported mortality.
- Formal use should cite the official report and its methods, not this researcher-created note alone.
