# Briefing for Apalachicola Riverkeeper

**Chipola River near Dolomite/Rocky Creek Mine and Arcosa Aggregates**
**Evidence cutoff:** September 2, 2026

## The short version

The reported mixed fish/reptile/alligator mortality has not yet been tied to a source by event-time water samples, tissue toxicology, necropsy, or an agency causal finding. The reach nevertheless warrants an urgent two-facility investigation. Dolomite has a direct permitted mining-lake discharge to the Chipola and a repeated historical Rocky Creek pathway. Arcosa, immediately downstream, has its own wastewater and stormwater pathways to the same reach, along with significant recent compliance findings.

The central technical failure is spatial and temporal: public monitoring does not bracket the event, the two closest recent ambient stations are five miles upstream and 6.7 miles downstream, and Dolomite’s 2012 biological study used a control only 80–180 meters upstream of its pipe along the mine frontage. No reviewed file supplies a modern reach-scale cumulative study of the two aggregate operations.

## Eight findings that matter most

1. **Dolomite’s compliance sample is not an event clearance.** Its July 2026 DMR showed no reported exceedance, but the permit requires only one monthly grab for flow, turbidity, TSS, and pH. It does not routinely monitor DO, temperature, nutrients, metals, fuels, pesticides, cyanotoxins, blasting residues, or whole-effluent toxicity.

2. **The 2015 Rocky Creek event was a Dolomite permit matter.** FDEP identified Dolomite Incorporated/FL0101192 and found wash/classifier water reaching the inactive quarry and Rocky Creek while bypassing D-002. The memo documents later controls; it does not transfer, release, or absolve the property or Baxter entities.

3. **The 2012 control station was upstream of the pipe, not above the operation.** GPS reconstruction places the biological control along the mine’s river frontage beside the pit setting. It can address an incremental D-001 effect during that sampling event; it cannot represent background above all Dolomite property influence or neighboring sources.

4. **The sampler-personnel concern remains partly unresolved.** The final file names Nathan Mulkey, Frank Butera, and Rick Abad and documents pre-event SCI/habitat qualifications for Abad. It does not say who chose the reaches, who had final design authority, or whether experienced Panhandle biologists were displaced. Those are records questions, not facts established by the public report.

5. **Arcosa is not merely a map label.** Its official permit authorizes two industrial surface-water discharges. FDEP found bypassing of its permitted weir and chronic pH/reporting problems in 2023; found an actual Chipola connection and other deficiencies in 2024; and entered a separate $20,500 mining/wetland settlement in February 2026.

6. **Arcosa’s 2026 mining order is substantial.** It records 0.13 acre of direct mixed-hardwood-wetland impact, 18.77 acres of secondary impact, and 9.47 UMAM functional-loss units; an offsite mitigation-property transfer; an already-constructed modified stormwater design; required cessation of unpermitted wetland mining; 7.61 forested and 15.89 wet-prairie/flatwoods mitigation credits; and $20,000 civil penalty plus $500 costs.

7. **Ambient results require criteria-aware interpretation.** Magnolia Road’s four 2025 total-nitrogen values produce an annual geometric mean of about **1.88 mg/L**, above the Panhandle West stream threshold of 0.67 mg/L; its May 22 E. coli was **1,553.1 MPN/100 mL**, above the 410 single-sample maximum. Because Magnolia is upstream of both mines, those results show upstream/regional loading and a bacterial episode—not mine causation. The SR 274 October 2023 total nitrogen was 1.58 mg/L, but one grab cannot establish annual nutrient impairment.

8. **The penalty-ceiling narrative needs correction.** Florida law in 2024 allowed administrative assessments up to $50,000. The $9,500 Dolomite settlement was not compelled by a $10,000 total ceiling. The public file does not yet show the penalty worksheet, economic-benefit adjustment, noncompliance-history adjustment, or settlement rationale.

## What to ask agencies for now

- Incident intake, complaint, hotline, State Watch Office, FWC mortality, photographs, GPS, samples, laboratory QA/QC, necropsy, and interagency records for the actual event window.
- Dolomite and Arcosa daily flows, raw DMR Part B/bench sheets, calibration, rainfall, pit/pond levels, pumping, transfers, bypasses, maintenance, spills, blasting, and construction logs.
- A field inspection of Dolomite D-001, the three Rocky Creek barriers and overflow elevations, Arcosa industrial D-001/D-002, Arcosa stormwater D-001, and the inter-mine/downstream reach.
- Dolomite’s April 2026 warning response/closure, OFS-1/OFS-2/Spillway 6 as-builts and startup logs, and the record supporting reduced monitoring.
- Arcosa’s pending FL0980722 renewal/modification, the response/closure for the 2023 and 2024 wastewater findings, current D-002 status, and all 2026 consent-order compliance submissions.
- The Outstanding Florida Water antidegradation demonstrations, baseline/ambient datasets, alternatives analyses, mixing assumptions, and cumulative-source analyses for both facilities.
- The 2012 Dolomite study’s site-selection emails, raw GPS, alternative reaches, assignment/chain of command, staff qualifications, QA review, and failed downstream Hester-Dendy follow-up.
- Dolomite’s 2024 penalty worksheet, economic-benefit and noncompliance-history calculations, peer review, and settlement authorization.

## Recommended field design

Use simultaneous or tightly synchronized stations at: a mine-independent upstream Chipola control; Dolomite D-001 before mixing; 100 m and 500 m below it; Rocky Creek above mine influence and below the historical route; the Chipola immediately above and below the Rocky Creek confluence; the reach above Arcosa; Arcosa industrial D-001 and stormwater D-001; the D-002 tributary if active; and downstream plume stations. Pair continuous DO/temperature/conductivity/turbidity sondes with event-triggered chemistry and FWC-directed carcass collection.

## What can and cannot be said publicly

**Supported:** two aggregate operations have documented pathways to the same Outstanding Florida Water reach; both have recent compliance histories; the public monitoring network is not capable of resolving this reported acute event; Dolomite’s 2012 control was not facility-independent; a cumulative two-operation review is warranted.

**Not supported yet:** that either facility was discharging at the relevant moment; that a permit limit was exceeded; that either facility caused a death; that a named employee intentionally selected a biased site; that enforcement outcomes resulted from political influence or corruption.
